<p class="lede">Research is only valuable if it's shared. Synthesize in one place, then turn evidence into insights people can act on.</p>

## Synthesizing research

<p class="section-intro">All synthesis happens in <strong>Dovetail</strong>, our single source of truth. Don't synthesize in private notes, whiteboards, or personal documents.</p>

<div class="research-steps">
<Steps>

1. ### Import your materials

   Upload transcripts, notes, and media into Dovetail, following your team's data-handling rules. Replace participant names with codes, and remove or redact personal information before synthesis.

2. ### Highlight and tag

   Mark meaningful moments — frustrations, successes, key quotes — with consistent tags such as "pain points," "workarounds," or "positive experience."

3. ### Group and cluster

   Look for themes across participants: similar words, repeated struggles, or patterns in behavior. Notes capture tone and body language; transcripts provide accuracy and detail. Together, they make patterns easier to spot.

4. ### Summarize insights

   Write clear, actionable insights supported by direct participant evidence. Focus on the "so what" — how findings should influence design or decision-making.

5. ### Check for bias

   Make sure your interpretation isn't overly shaped by your own assumptions or a single participant's story.

</Steps>
</div>

## Sharing research

<p class="section-intro">Balance clarity, respect for participants, and impact in every output.</p>

Every research output should include:

1. **Purpose** — Why we did this research
2. **Methods** — Who we spoke to and how
3. **Key themes and insights** — Supported by anonymized participant quotes
4. **Opportunities or recommendations** — How findings can improve services or inform decisions
5. **Limitations** — Be transparent about sample size, scope, or constraints

### Output formats

<div class="compact-cards">
<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Insight summaries" icon="document">
    One-page briefs highlighting key findings and recommendations. Can be created and shared directly through Dovetail.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Slide decks" icon="laptop">
    Concise presentations with quotes, themes, and visuals — ideal for stakeholder meetings.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Visual artifacts" icon="star">
    Journey maps, personas, or service blueprints that illustrate complex user experiences at a glance.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Reports" icon="list-format">
    Detailed documentation for complex projects or cross-agency work where depth is needed.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>
</div>

<Aside type="note" title="Accessibility matters">
Keep all presentations and reports screen-reader friendly. Use clear, high-contrast visuals, provide alt text for images and diagrams, and write summaries in plain language for diverse audiences.
</Aside>

## Quick reference checklist

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="Before research">
    - Peer review completed
    - Design Director approval secured
    - Budget and compensation approved
</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Before session">
    - Session piloted with internal team members to check timing and flow
    - Consent form sent and confirmed
    - Tech check completed (Zoom / Teams)
    - Notetaker and observers briefed, renamed, and muted
    - Participants reminded not to share PII
</TabItem>
<TabItem label="During session">
    - Confirm consent (recorded or notes-only)
    - Acknowledge observers and reassure *"just you and me"*
    - Ask clear, open, neutral questions
    - Respect time and use silence wisely
</TabItem>
<TabItem label="After session">
    - Review the recording for personal information (within 24 hours for health-related studies)
    - Replace names with participant codes, and redact or remove personal details
    - Keep originals only in restricted storage, and delete them on your retention schedule
    - Mark session as **Completed** in UserInterviews
</TabItem>
<TabItem label="When sharing">
    - Share themes, insights, and de-identified quotes only
    - Never share public links to research data
    - Use participant language to keep findings authentic
    - Tailor the format (summary, deck, map) to your audience
    - Make reports accessible — clear visuals, alt text, plain language
</TabItem>
</Tabs>
