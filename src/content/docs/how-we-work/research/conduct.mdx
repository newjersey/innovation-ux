## Recruiting participants

<Aside type="note">
**Default rule: always start with UserInterviews.com.** Only move to secondary or tertiary channels when UserInterviews is not a good fit for your study.
</Aside>

<div class="research-steps">
<Steps>

1. ### Primary: UserInterviews.com

   A recruiting platform handles everything in one place: recruitment, screening, scheduling, communication, and incentives.

   - Use screening criteria when setting up the platform
   - Communicate with participants within the platform to verify their fit for the study

2. ### Secondary: Community and partner organizations

   Use when working with a very specific group that's hard to reach otherwise — for example, disability advocacy organizations, LGBTQIA+ organizations, or community-based organizations.

   Working with partners can be time-intensive, so plan ahead. Talk with the research team before you contact any partner organization about recruiting.

3. ### Tertiary: Independent and manual methods

   Use sparingly and with intention. Options include:
   - Targeted Facebook groups *(always contact moderators first)*
   - The business.nj.gov newsletter
   - LinkedIn ads *(with C+E Lab support)*
   - Multilingual partners or services for non-English speakers

   <Aside type="caution">
   Manual recruitment often attracts participants more motivated by incentives than topic relevance. Screening phone calls are strongly encouraged before any interview.
   </Aside>

</Steps>
</div>

## Communicating with participants

All communication with participants should happen through **UserInterviews.com**, including screeners and scheduling.

### Informed consent

<p class="section-intro">Participants must complete a consent form before research begins.</p>

Every consent form should:

- Explain the purpose and format of the research
- Clarify confidentiality (personal information stays private)
- Request consent to record, with an option to participate without recording
- Explain who can see recordings, how long you keep them, and when they're deleted
- Explain that any excerpts shared outside the research team will have personal information removed
- Remind participants they can withdraw or change consent at any time

Our team: use the current consent form template in the [Research folder on the shared drive](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1CK19sPsEhp1t_Z6627u6tG468ZQnnL8l).

<Aside type="caution" title="Special consent required for">
- **Minors** — parent or guardian signature required
- **Public-facing quotes** — for example, use in a press release
</Aside>

## Collecting and managing data

<Aside type="danger" title="Personal information must always be stored securely">
Store recordings and anything that identifies a participant only in approved, access-restricted storage that only the immediate research team can open. Never use personal devices or personal accounts for research data.
</Aside>

<div class="research-steps">
<Steps>

1. ### Store sensitive data

   All personal information — names, contact details, recordings, transcripts — goes in your project's restricted storage. Never store participant data in general project folders, personal drives, chat tools, or open shared spaces.

2. ### Record sessions

   - **Remote sessions:** Record through Zoom or Teams, integrated via UserInterviews. Ask participants to change their display name to a first name, so full names stay out of transcripts.
   - **In-person sessions:** Move recordings from the recording device to restricted storage promptly, then delete them from the device.

   Always remind participants not to enter, display, or say personal information during the session. If they do, note the timestamp so you can remove it later.

3. ### Handle accidental PII capture

   If personal information is accidentally captured in a recording:
   - Review the recording soon after the session (within 24 hours for health-related studies)
   - Remove or redact the personal information before analysis or sharing
   - Keep only the de-identified version for synthesis, and delete the original on your retention schedule

4. ### Prepare recordings for synthesis

   De-identify recordings and transcripts before synthesis: replace names with participant codes (P1, P2) and remove personal details. Useful tools:
   - **Clipchamp** — Microsoft's web-based video editor for trimming audio and video
   - **Windows Voice Recorder** — built-in tool for recording and trimming audio

5. ### Share findings responsibly

   Share themes, insights, and de-identified quotes only. Never share full recordings, transcripts, or participant names outside the research team, and never share public links to research data.

</Steps>
</div>

## Conducting interviews

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="Before">
Begin every session by setting expectations and building trust:

- Introduce yourself and your role
- Explain the purpose of the session in plain language
- Confirm consent (recording or notes-only)
- Reassure participants they can skip any question or stop at any time
- Ask if they need any accommodations

<Aside type="note" title="When observers are present">
Acknowledge that others may be on the call, then reassure the participant: *"You may notice some other names on the call. They're here to quietly observe or take notes — today's session is really just between you and me."*
</Aside>
</TabItem>
<TabItem label="During">
<div class="compact-cards">
<CardGrid>
  <Card title="One question at a time" icon="approve-check-circle">
    Avoid double-barreled questions. Ask about one thing, get a response, then move on.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Stay neutral" icon="information">
    Don't lead participants toward a particular answer. Use language that leaves the door open.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Keep it open-ended" icon="open-book">
    Encourage richer responses. Avoid yes/no questions wherever possible.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Use their language" icon="translate">
    Mirror the terms participants use instead of imposing jargon or internal terminology.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>
</div>
</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Observers">
- Stay **off camera** and **on mute** at all times
- Do **not** use chat during the session
- Change your display name to **Notetaker** or **Observer**
- Your role is to listen — let the facilitator lead
</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Ending">
- Ask if participants have any final thoughts
- Thank them sincerely for their time and input
- Share next steps — how findings may be used or when they might hear from you
- If they opted in to future research through your recruiting platform, let them know they may hear from you again. Don't recruit them for future studies directly.
</TabItem>
<TabItem label="After">
<Aside type="danger" title="Required after every session">
Mark the participant as **"Completed"** in UserInterviews. If an interaction makes you uncomfortable, end the session, contact UserInterviews support, and do not mark it complete until after investigation.
</Aside>

- Review the recording for personal information (within 24 hours for health-related studies)
- De-identify, then upload to your research repository
- Debrief with your notetaker
</TabItem>
</Tabs>
